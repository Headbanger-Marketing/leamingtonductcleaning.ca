Does the cooling system leave rooms feeling damp?

For furnace and AC cleaning in Leamington, identify the equipment type, note any cooling or airflow concerns, and photograph visible dust around accessible components before requesting a scope. If the property has an evaporator coil that remains wet during cooling, ask how coil access and cleaning will be handled. A blower assembly, coil, and heat exchanger face are different components; confirm which are included and whether the equipment’s age or condition affects what can safely be cleaned.

Are ventilation fans weak after a home has been closed?

For HRV and ventilation cleaning in Leamington, first determine whether the property is occupied year-round or opened seasonally, then note which fans and ventilation units are present. If an HRV has been shut down for an extended period, check the core, screens, and condensate path for visible debris or standing water where safely accessible. Ask for separate scope details for the HRV and exhaust ducts behind bathroom fans or range hoods, since one does not automatically include the other.

Does a musty smell appear when the system starts?

For duct sanitizing or deodorizing in Leamington, identify the specific odour, when it occurs, and whether it is limited to one room or appears at several registers. If a property has been closed for a season, had a damp basement, or has persistent cooking odours, those details can help focus the inspection. Ask whether the duct system will be cleaned first, which treatment is proposed, and what product information and use instructions will be provided before deciding whether treatment suits the situation.

Information for Your Leamington Quote

  • The work you need

    Ask for a scope covering supply and return runs, vacuum containment, brushing, and which accessible sections can be photographed before and after. Compare scopes from the dryer connection to the exterior hood, including elbows, concealed access, and whether airflow will be measured afterward. Clarify whether the blower assembly, evaporator coil, and heat exchanger face are included, and how equipment age or access affects the method.

  • The wider system

    Separate HRV core, screen, and condensate checks from cleaning bathroom or kitchen exhaust ducts; seasonal use can affect the inspection plan. Describe the odour and its timing, then verify that cleaning comes first and request product details and label-based use instructions.

  • Related work to mention

    Share duct sketches or photos and compare scopes for tracing leaks, sealing joints with mastic, and replacing crushed or detached sections. Provide equipment lists, drawings, access rules, and operating windows to compare plans for rooftop units, long runs, scheduling, and records.

Ask the responding provider for a written scope, any exclusions, applicable qualifications and insurance, the expected visit window, and the terms of any warranty. Keep the estimate and invoice together. Published service guides explain common work; they do not replace an inspection of your property.
